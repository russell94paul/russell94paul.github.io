Every claim carries its evidence, or says it has none

CELL//OS is a research project in building systems that refuse to fool themselves. This page describes the method: how a result is allowed to count. It also lists what has actually been measured, kept separate from what is designed or still an idea.

The method

Six rules that decide whether a result counts

1 · Decide the pass criteria before running

Every gate states its pass criteria, inputs and prohibitions before it runs. The run happens once, against a hash-pinned commit, from a clean snapshot. No rerunning until green.

2 · Prove the instrument can fail

Every acceptance check is first shown to fire on a known-bad case (a negative control). A test that has never failed is a test that might not be looking.

3 · Keep "zero" separate from "not measured"

Results use five verdicts: PASS, FAIL, UNMEASURABLE, ERROR, NOT-RUN. A blind instrument's silence is never reported as a clean result.

4 · Isolate causes one factor at a time

When a run fails, each suspected cause is introduced alone, in its own disposable snapshot. In the 1,000-test verification this confirmed 18 of 19 suspected environmental failures as environmental.

5 · Replicate independently

A second implementation is written from the specification alone, and the two must agree. Where they don't, the specification is the thing under suspicion.

6 · Seal the evidence

Artefacts are sealed with SHA-256 manifests and external checksum sidecars, and superseded rather than overwritten. A result can be re-checked byte for byte, later, by someone else.

Built, designed, or an idea

Four labels, never blurred

Implemented

Built and usable in a named context: the evaluation framework, evidence-gated completion, sealed evidence, the local evidence workspace.

Demonstrated

Explorable on fictional data in your browser: the CELL-LIFT demo and the CELL-Q lab.

Designed

Specified, not yet proven: the immutable run ledger, the canonical ontology and the provider-neutral adapters.

Research

Hypotheses: SIHRE as the reasoning kernel of every Operative, adaptive team composition, cross-product capability sharing.

Approval is not implementation, implementation is not verification, and a demo is not validation. The full feature ledger is on the Status page, with definitions here.

How this was built

Directed AI agents, held to the same standard

Much of the code and research text in CELL//OS was produced by AI coding and research agents (Claude Code, Codex) working under my direction. My part was the part this page is about: deciding the questions, setting the evidence standards, designing the gates and controls, and accepting or rejecting each result. Agent output counts as a candidate until it passes the same gates as anything else. That is also why the record above includes the failures.
